# 2026-11-20 PROVE — Q597 G1b: the no-repeat law (PROVED, at every size)

Full write-up: `proofs/2026-11-20-Q597-no-repeat.tex` (+ pdf). Code: `proofs/code-1120-Q597/`.

## Theorem
Let the board be a Purbhoo mosaic. Then (N) holds: every back edge of a nest-A frame cell faces either the next nest rhombus or the region boundary (Lemma N). So, in every terminating Purbhoo journey toward B or C (engine rule S1–S4), no non-rhombus tile created by a step is removed by a later step.

**Corollaries.**
- The 11-16 no-repeat law holds: no triangle moves twice.
- The 11-16 direction law holds:
  - B lowers x₂ and C raises x₃ on moved 0-triangles;
  - B raises y₂ and C lowers y₃ on moved 1-triangles.
- G1b of 11-19 c2 is closed.

## The potential
Work in Λ₀ × Λ₁, not in the plane. Put η_B(a,b) = b and η_C(a,b) = a+b. For a tile t, let

ψ(t) = (min over t of −η(P₁), min over t of η(P₀)),

and let μ_k = ψ(R_k) be the value on the rhombus before step k. Along every journey:
- **MU:** μ is componentwise non-increasing.
- **REM:** every removed non-rhombus tile has ψ ≤ μ_k.
- **CRE:** every created non-rhombus tile has ψ_i ≥ (μ_{k+1})_i + 1 for some i.

These three together give no-revisit in one line.

The 11-19 c2 scan found no single plane functional that works (0/3491). Two lattice heights read as a product order do work.

## Proof skeleton
1. **Rhombus class.** The rhombus is always s₀ × s₁ with the same coordinate vector u, and η(u) ≠ 0. This holds at the start (u = (0,1)) and is preserved by good steps.
2. **Local table (computer-assisted, exhaustive, board-free).**
   - Every step hexagon containing such a rhombus that passes the x-rule is listed.
   - Good configurations satisfy MU, REM, CRE, DL and W1.
   - Bad configurations (8 of 32) have their across edge on the boundary, so the tie-break kills them whenever there is a second candidate.
   - Two independent enumerators agree 36/36: one via Lemma-hex normal forms, one by BFS that does not use Lemma hex. The a-rows for B were also checked by hand.
3. **Undo lemma (finite check).** After a good step G, the only bad configuration compatible with image(G) is image(G) itself, and its rotation inverts G.
4. **Start.** Bad configurations need both back edges of R interior. At k = 0, (N) says those edges face a rhombus or the boundary, so no bad configuration can be a candidate.
5. **Every step is good.** Suppose a step is bad. It must then be the unique candidate, so it is the undo of the previous step. The deterministic journey becomes 2-periodic and never terminates, a contradiction.

## Verification
- MU, REM and CRE hold at every step: 3881/3881 B/C and 898/898 B/B-second journey records at ≤5.
- **Lesion:** with the step order reversed, REM and CRE fail in every journey.
- Real candidates: 40131/40131 are in the table, and 10395/10395 bad candidates are undos. None occurs at k = 0.
- Size-6 fixed subsample (chunks 0–2 of 24): 2681/2681 B/C and 794/794 B/B-second journeys pass. Every bad candidate is an undo.
