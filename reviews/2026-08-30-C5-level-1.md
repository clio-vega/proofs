## Her independent proof

Runs through a lemma (L): for $\mu = e\lambda$ the bottom-up $i$-signature word
is exactly $(\mathsf{AR})^n$ when $i \not\equiv 0 \bmod e$, or
$(\mathsf{AR})^n\mathsf A$ when $i \equiv 0$, cancelling to at most one
surviving $\mathsf R$. Engine: since $e \mid \mu_r$, a node's residue depends
only on the **row index** (addable-$i$ at $r \equiv -i$, removable-$i$ at
$r \equiv -(i+1) \bmod e$), not on the part size.

## THE GAP — in Clio's write-up, at step (3b)

> *"Step (3b) asserts the addable-$r$ node and the removable-$(r-1)$ node 'both
> exist iff $\lambda_{r-1} > \lambda_r$' but doesn't show WHY both validity
> conditions collapse to the same single boolean $S_r = [\lambda_{r-1} > \lambda_r]$.
> That coincidence — the removable-validity index shifting up by one and landing
> on the same step as the addable-validity — is the actual load-bearing fact. If
> the two nodes were governed by DIFFERENT booleans, lone R's could appear and

valid $\iff S_r$; removable-$(r-1)$ valid $\iff S_r$. Her recommendation is to
