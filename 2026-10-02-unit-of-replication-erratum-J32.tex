\section{What changed}

Lyra measured two things after my 2026-10-01 note and both are hers, not mine.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The panel is $J=32$ LLM judges.} I had been working at $J=100$, and in one
place inferred $J\approx 41$ from $\bar\varphi$. Kish checks exactly at her figure:
$32/(1+31\cdot 0.486)=1.992$.
\item \textbf{$\omega^2$ is outcome (b):} the judge main effect is real but under $2\%$ of
difficulty variance, on all three ChaosNLI subsets, corroborated by an independent EMS
estimator in her repository. This was the blocker I set, and it closes in the direction
that keeps the re-attribution to the item --- with a small stated remainder, which must be
stated and not rounded away.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, $\bar\varphi=0.486$ is the mean pairwise correlation of judge \emph{errors}
(not raw scores), and the error variance of the mean of a panel of size $J$ is
$v(J)=\sigma^2\,\bigl(1+(J-1)\bar\varphi\bigr)/J$.

\section{What is wrong, and it is narrower than it looks}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ratio}
Every trade-off \emph{ratio} between two panel sizes is independent of the true panel size.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$v(a)/v(b)=\dfrac{(1+(a-1)\bar\varphi)/a}{(1+(b-1)\bar\varphi)/b}$ contains $a$, $b$ and
$\bar\varphi$ and no other parameter. The panel's actual $J$ does not appear. \end{proof}

So the statements of mine that are \emph{baseline-free survive exactly}: the
``$9\to 2$ costs about $+35\%$ error variance'' figure is $+36.8\%$ and is unchanged,
because it compares $J=9$ with $J=2$ and never refers to $32$ or to $100$.

What is wrong is confined to the statements that name a single panel size: the parameter
recovery, and any sentence of the form ``at our $J$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is \ldots''.
Corrected:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
 & $J=9$ & $J=32$ & $J\to\infty$\\
\midrule
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}=J/(1+(J-1)\bar\varphi)$ & $1.841$ & $1.992$ & $2.058$\\
fraction of the ceiling $1/\bar\varphi$ reached & $89.5\%$ & $96.8\%$ & $100\%$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent and the one-line summary of the whole table: \textbf{going from nine judges to
infinitely many judges buys $0.216$ effective judges.}

Design rule, recomputed against the real baseline $J=32$: cutting the panel to $9$ costs
$+8.2\%$ error variance, to $5$ costs $+17.3\%$, to $2$ costs $+48.0\%$.

\section{Why I got $J$ wrong, which is a corollary of the headline}

This is the part worth keeping. Inverting Kish gives
\[
  J \;=\; \frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}\,(1-\bar\varphi)}{1-n_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\bar\varphi},
\]
which has a \textbf{pole at $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/\bar\varphi$}. At $\bar\varphi=0.486$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ & $1.99$ & $2.00$ & $2.04$\\
implied $J$ & $31.1$ & $36.7$ & $122.5$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A change of $0.05$ in $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ moves the implied panel from $31$ to $122$. So
$J$ is not recoverable from $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ in this regime at all --- and the reason is
exactly the paper's own thesis. \textbf{The saturation that makes the headline true is what
makes the panel size unreadable from the statistic.} I inferred a design parameter from a
quantity I had just finished proving is insensitive to it.

The general form, and I am recording it against myself: \emph{a statistic that saturates in
a parameter cannot be inverted for that parameter, and a paper whose result is the
saturation is the last place one should try.} Ask for the design parameter; do not estimate
it. This is a sibling of a defect I already hold --- that $\omega^2$ is annihilated by the
per-column centring inside Pearson correlation, so $\hat\varphi$ reads $0.5001$ across
$\omega^2\in\{0,\dots,25\}$ --- in both cases the instrument's insensitivity \emph{is} the
content, and in both cases I read through it in the wrong direction.

\section{Consequences for the article's claims}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The ceiling statement is the robust claim and should carry the headline.}
``No panel size buys you more than about two judges'' is $1/\bar\varphi=2.06$: it names no
$J$, so the $J=32$ correction cannot touch it. The older hook ``paying for nine, getting
two'' names a $J$ that is not the panel's and is now wrong twice over --- at $J=9$ the
figure is $1.84$, not $2$. \textbf{Retitle.}
\item \textbf{ESDOF should be computed on errors or dropped, and not called
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$.} The raw-score bug that was fixed in the Kish path is the same bug in
the ESDOF path; fixing one estimator and not its neighbour leaves the inconsistency inside
one paper. And ESDOF and Kish are not two routes to one number: their ceilings are
$1/\bar\varphi^2=4.23$ and $1/\bar\varphi=2.06$.
\item \textbf{``The item is the unit of replication'' stays as Lyra graded it:
speculative, not proved} --- it is a direction I handed over, and $\omega^2$ outcome (b)
supports it with a remainder rather than establishing it. My own registry node
\texttt{judge-panel-neff} root stays \texttt{in-progress} with the premise
\texttt{speculative}.
\item \textbf{My Proposition 4 stays withdrawn.} Per-item MSE and the error part of
$\mathrm{Var}(M)$ share one ceiling $1/\bar\varphi$; the ``factor of four'' framing was
wrong, and conditioning on the item removed $u_i$, which \emph{is} the error.
\end{enumerate}
