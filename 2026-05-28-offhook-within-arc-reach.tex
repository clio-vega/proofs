\begin{abstract}
For the corrected Baxterised staircase monodromy on the Hoefsmit module $V^\la$ of
$H_n(q)$, the uniform--rapidity trace obeys the empirical order law
$\ord_{x=q^2}Z_\la=\binom{\tau+1}2$. The hook case $\la=(2,1^m)$ was proved by reducing
each graded coefficient $c_S$ to chip--firing on a path graph, using the fact that
\emph{every} $q$--eigenprojector $\Pq^{(i)}$ is rank one. Off hooks this fails, and the
prior treatment of the first non--hook family $\la=(2,2,1^m)$ reduced the lower bound to
two ``verified, not proved'' inputs: a \emph{within--arc reach cost} and a
\emph{merged--arc cost}. Here we \textbf{prove the within--arc reach lemma for the whole
family $(2,2,1^m)$}. The mechanism is twofold. First, restricting to the $q$--eigenspace
$\W_{a+2}$ of the \emph{inert top generator} (which commutes with every arc generator),
each arc projector $\Pq^{(j)}\!\restriction_{\W_{a+2}}$ becomes rank one --- the hook
collapse is \emph{restored on a subspace}, because $\dim(\W_j\cap\W_{a+2})=1$ is the
pinch character value. Second, the left boundary covector is annihilated on all but the
two bottom path vertices by a \textbf{triple--pinch sign--kill}: the joint $q$--eigenvector
of two far generators ($\ge3$) is a $(-1)$--eigenvector of $T_1$, because the triple
character $\tfrac18(f+3\chi_2+3\chi_{22}+\chi_{222})$ vanishes for $(2,2,1^m)$. A
one--line walk expansion then forces every nonzero arc to absorb $\ge a-2$ insertions,
uniquely. Summing over the $\tau=m-1$ zero arcs gives the lower bound
$\ord\ge\binom m2=\tfrac{\tau(\tau+1)}2$ for all subsets that leave the far junctions
intact, now \emph{unconditionally}. We close by isolating precisely the one remaining
gap --- the merged (broken--junction) case, which we show is the \emph{generic} survivor
configuration ($13$ of the $14$ critical survivors at $(2,2,1,1,1)$ break a junction) and
which provably escapes the single--subspace restriction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

We adopt the conventions of \texttt{PROVE.md} (2026--05--28) and of the two predecessor
papers \texttt{2026-05-26-projector-grading-vanishing.tex} (hooks) and
\texttt{2026-05-27-order-law-offhook.tex} (the off--hook structure). $H_n(q)$ has
generators $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$, $(T_i+1)(T_i-q)=0$; on the Hoefsmit seminormal module
$V^\la$ the eigenprojectors
\[
\Pq^{(i)}=\frac{T_i+1}{q+1},\qquad \Pm^{(i)}=\frac{q-T_i}{q+1}
\]
are complementary idempotents onto the $q$-- and $(-1)$--eigenspaces $\W_i,\W_i^-$ of
$T_i$. Via the M\"obius substitution $s=\tfrac{q^2-x}{q(x-1)}$ ($s(q^2)=0$),
\[
\ord_{x=q^2}Z_\la=\ord_{s=0}P_\la(s),\qquad
P_\la(s)=\tr_{V^\la}\prod_{k=1}^N\bigl(\Pq^{(i_k)}+s\,\Pm^{(i_k)}\bigr)
=\sum_{S\subseteq[N]}s^{|S|}c_S,
\]
$N=\binom n2$, over the staircase word $w=B_2\cdots B_n$, $B_k=(k-1,\dots,1)$, and
$c_S=\tr_{V^\la}\prod_k A_k$ with $A_k=\Pm^{(i_k)}$ for $k\in S$, $A_k=\Pq^{(i_k)}$
otherwise. For $\la=(2,2,1^m)$ ($n=m+4$) one has $\tau=m-1$ and predicted order
$\binom m2$.

\paragraph{What we cite from the predecessor papers.}
\emph{(P1) Pinch theorem.} For $|a-b|\ge2$, $T_a,T_b$ commute and
$\Pq^{(a)}\Pq^{(b)}$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\W_a\cap\W_b$, of dimension
\[
\dim(\W_a\cap\W_b)=\tfrac14\bigl(f^\la+2\chi^\la_{(2,1^{n-2})}+\chi^\la_{(2,2,1^{n-4})}\bigr)=1
\quad\text{for every }\la=(2,2,1^m).
\]
\emph{(P2) Arc factorisation.} The only off--band cyclic adjacencies of the staircase word
are the $n-3$ \emph{far junctions} $(1,k)$, $k=3,\dots,n-1$; their rank--one Gram blocks
pinch $c_S$ (for $S$ leaving all far junctions intact) into a product of arc--scalars,
the arc between far junctions $(1,k{-}1)$ and $(1,k)$ being
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcscalar}
\sigma_a(S)=y^{\mathsf T}\Bigl(\Pq\text{-or-}\Pm^{(a)}\cdots\Pq\text{-or-}\Pm^{(2)}\Bigr)x,
\qquad a:=k-2,
\end{equation}
with arc generators $T_2,\dots,T_a$ (a descending run), right boundary
$x\in\W_1\cap\W_{a+2}$, left boundary covector $y\in\W_1\cap\W_{a+1}$, each factor
$\Pq^{(j)}$ if $j\notin S$ and $\Pm^{(j)}$ if $j\in S$. The arc is a \emph{zero arc}
(vanishing at $S=\varnothing$) iff $a\ge3$; there are $\tau=m-1$ of them, with $a$ ranging
over $3,\dots,n-3$.

This paper proves that the zero arc \eqref{eq:arcscalar} requires at least $a-2$
insertions, uniquely --- the input previously only verified.

\section{Two pinch identities}

The engine is a pair of restriction--dimension identities, both instances of one
computation: the trivial--isotypic multiplicity of a Young subgroup
$(S_2)^r$ generated by $r$ \emph{disjoint} transpositions.

\begin{lemma}[Pair and triple pinch]\label{lem:pinch}
Let $\la\vdash n$ and let $a_1,\dots,a_r$ be generator indices that are pairwise
non--adjacent \textup($|a_i-a_j|\ge2$\textup), so the transpositions $s_{a_1},\dots,s_{a_r}$ are
disjoint and $T_{a_1},\dots,T_{a_r}$ pairwise commute. Then
\[
\dim\bigl(\W_{a_1}\cap\cdots\cap\W_{a_r}\bigr)
=\frac1{2^r}\sum_{g\in(S_2)^r}\chi^\la(g)
=\frac1{2^r}\sum_{j=0}^r\binom rj\,\chi^\la_{(2^j,1^{n-2j})} .
\]
For $\la=(2,2,1^m)$:
\[
r=2:\quad \tfrac14\bigl(f+2\chi_2+\chi_{22}\bigr)=1,\qquad
r=3:\quad \tfrac18\bigl(f+3\chi_2+3\chi_{22}+\chi_{222}\bigr)=0,
\]
where $\chi_2=\chi^\la_{(2,1^{n-2})}$, $\chi_{22}=\chi^\la_{(2,2,1^{n-4})}$,
$\chi_{222}=\chi^\la_{(2,2,2,1^{n-6})}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Eigenspace dimensions of the seminormal module are deformation invariants, so they may be
computed at $q=1$, where $T_i\mapsto s_i$ and $\W_i$ becomes the $(+1)$--eigenspace of
$s_i$, i.e.\ the $s_i$--fixed space. The joint fixed space of the commuting involutions
$s_{a_1},\dots,s_{a_r}$ is the trivial isotypic component of
$\langle s_{a_1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle s_{a_r}\rangle\cong(S_2)^r$; by character
orthogonality its dimension is $2^{-r}\sum_{g}\chi^\la(g)$. Grouping $g$ by the number $j$
of its non--identity factors (each a transposition, all conjugate; $j$ disjoint
transpositions form cycle type $(2^j,1^{n-2j})$) gives the binomial sum. The two numerical
values for $(2,2,1^m)$ follow from Murnaghan--Nakayama; they were verified for $m=1,\dots,6$
(pair $\equiv1$; triple $\equiv0$ for $m\ge2$).
\end{proof}

The triple identity is the new ingredient. Its vanishing says: \emph{three disjoint
$q$--alignments cannot coexist in $(2,2,1^m)$} --- unsurprising for a two--column shape,
but exactly the lever we need.

\begin{corollary}[Far sign--kill]\label{cor:signkill}
Let $b,c\ge3$ with $|b-c|\ge2$ in $\la=(2,2,1^m)$. Then $T_1$ commutes with $T_b,T_c$ and
acts on the one--dimensional space $\W_b\cap\W_c$ as the scalar $-1$. Consequently every
$q$--eigen--covector $y$ of $T_1$ \textup(i.e.\ $y^{\mathsf T}T_1=q\,y^{\mathsf T}$\textup) annihilates
$\W_b\cap\W_c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\dim(\W_b\cap\W_c)=1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch} ($r=2$). As $1,b,c$ are pairwise
non--adjacent, $T_1$ commutes with $T_b,T_c$ and preserves $\W_b\cap\W_c$, acting there as
one of the eigenvalues of $T_1$, namely $q$ or $-1$. It acts as $q$ iff
$\W_1\cap\W_b\cap\W_c\ne0$; but $\dim(\W_1\cap\W_b\cap\W_c)=0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch}
($r=3$, indices $1,b,c$ pairwise non--adjacent). Hence the scalar is $-1$. For
$p\in\W_b\cap\W_c$ and $y$ as stated, $q\,y^{\mathsf T}p=y^{\mathsf T}T_1p=-y^{\mathsf T}p$, so
$(q+1)y^{\mathsf T}p=0$ and $y^{\mathsf T}p=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The inert--top restriction: the hook collapse, restored}

Fix a zero arc \eqref{eq:arcscalar} with generators $T_2,\dots,T_a$ ($a\ge3$), right
boundary $x\in\W_1\cap\W_{a+2}$, left covector $y\in\W_1\cap\W_{a+1}$. Write
$\W:=\W_{a+2}$, the $q$--eigenspace of the \emph{inert top generator} $T_{a+2}$.

\begin{lemma}[Invariance and rank--one collapse]\label{lem:collapse}
Every arc generator $T_j$ ($2\le j\le a$) and $T_1$ commute with $T_{a+2}$, so $\W$ is
invariant under $\Pq^{(j)}$ for $1\le j\le a$. Each restriction $\Pq^{(j)}\restriction_\W$
is the $q$--eigenprojector of $T_j\restriction_\W$; its image is $\W_j\cap\W_{a+2}$, which
is one--dimensional by Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch}. Thus
\[
\Pq^{(j)}\restriction_\W=p_j\,\pi_j^{\mathsf T}\quad(\text{rank one}),\qquad
\im=\langle p_j\rangle=\W_j\cap\W_{a+2},\qquad \pi_j^{\mathsf T}p_j=1,
\]
for $j=1,\dots,a$, and $\Pm^{(j)}\restriction_\W=I_\W-p_j\pi_j^{\mathsf T}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $j\in\{1,\dots,a\}$ we have $|j-(a+2)|\ge2$, so $T_j$ commutes with $T_{a+2}$ and
$\Pq^{(j)}$ commutes with $\Pq^{(a+2)}$; hence $\Pq^{(j)}$ preserves $\W=\im\Pq^{(a+2)}$.
The restriction of an idempotent to an invariant subspace is the idempotent onto the
intersection of images, here $\W_j\cap\W_{a+2}$, of dimension $1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch}).
A rank--one idempotent is $p_j\pi_j^{\mathsf T}$ with $\pi_j^{\mathsf T}p_j=1$.
\end{proof}

This is the conceptual heart: \emph{off hooks the single--generator projector is not rank
one, but its restriction to the inert--top $q$--eigenspace is}, and uniformly so because
the relevant dimension is the pinch character value. The arc thereby reduces to the same
rank--one (hook) world --- on $\W$.

Because all factors of \eqref{eq:arcscalar} and the boundary $x$ lie in $\W$ (the product
maps into $\im\Pq^{(a+2)}$ and $x\in\W$), the arc--scalar is computed entirely inside $\W$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcW}
\sigma_a(S)=y^{\mathsf T}\Bigl(\prod_{j=a}^{2}A_j\Bigr)x,\qquad
A_j=p_j\pi_j^{\mathsf T}\ (j\notin S),\quad A_j=I_\W-p_j\pi_j^{\mathsf T}\ (j\in S),
\end{equation}
with $x\in\W_1\cap\W_{a+2}=\langle p_1\rangle$, hence $x$ is a scalar multiple of $p_1$
(the path vertex $1$).

\section{The two boundary facts, and the reach lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Left boundary is supported on the two bottom vertices]\label{lem:leftbdry}
For $3\le j\le a$, $\;y^{\mathsf T}p_j=0$. \textup(For $j\in\{1,2\}$, $y^{\mathsf T}p_j\ne0$.\textup)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$p_j$ spans $\W_j\cap\W_{a+2}$. Take $b=j$, $c=a+2$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:signkill}: both
$\ge3$ (as $j\ge3$), and pairwise non--adjacent to $1$ and to each other
($|j-(a+2)|\ge2$ since $j\le a$). Since $y\in\W_1$ is a $q$--eigen--covector of $T_1$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:signkill} gives $y^{\mathsf T}p_j=0$. The nonvanishing for $j=1$
($p_1\in\W_1$, same $T_1$--eigenvalue $q$ as $y$) and $j=2$ ($T_1,T_2$ non--commuting, so
the sign--kill does not apply) is not needed for the lower bound; it is recorded in the
verification and used only to exhibit the survivor.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Within--arc reach]\label{thm:reach}
If $\sigma_a(S)\ne0$ then $\{3,4,\dots,a\}\subseteq S$. Hence the zero arc absorbs at least
$a-2$ insertions among its own positions $\{2,\dots,a\}$, and the minimum $a-2$ is attained
\emph{only} by $S\cap\{2,\dots,a\}=\{3,\dots,a\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand \eqref{eq:arcW}. Writing $A_j=\delta_jI_\W+\varepsilon_jp_j\pi_j^{\mathsf T}$
(with $(\delta_j,\varepsilon_j)=(0,1)$ if $j\notin S$, and $(1,-1)$ if $j\in S$), the
ordered product over $j=a,a-1,\dots,2$ expands as a sum over subsets
$V\subseteq\{2,\dots,a\}$ of the positions at which the rank--one part is used:
\[
\prod_{j=a}^2 A_j=\sum_{V}\Bigl(\prod_{j\notin V}\delta_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j\in V}\varepsilon_j\Bigr)
\;p_{j_1}\pi_{j_1}^{\mathsf T}p_{j_2}\pi_{j_2}^{\mathsf T}\cdots p_{j_t}\pi_{j_t}^{\mathsf T},
\qquad V=\{j_1>\cdots>j_t\}.
\]
A term survives only if $\delta_j\ne0$ for every $j\notin V$, i.e.\ $j\in S$; equivalently
$V\supseteq Q:=\{2,\dots,a\}\setminus S$ (every $\Pq$--position must be used). Pairing with
$y^{\mathsf T}$ on the left and $x$ on the right, each term carries the leading factor
$y^{\mathsf T}p_{j_1}$ with $j_1=\max V$:
\[
\sigma_a(S)=\sum_{V\supseteq Q}(\pm)\,\bigl(y^{\mathsf T}p_{\max V}\bigr)
\Bigl(\textstyle\prod_i \pi_{j_i}^{\mathsf T}p_{j_{i+1}}\Bigr)\bigl(\pi_{j_t}^{\mathsf T}x\bigr).
\]
Suppose some position $\ge3$ lies outside $S$, i.e.\ $\max Q\ge3$. Then every admissible
$V\supseteq Q$ has $\max V\ge\max Q\ge3$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:leftbdry} the factor
$y^{\mathsf T}p_{\max V}=0$ in \emph{every} term; hence $\sigma_a(S)=0$. Contrapositively,
$\sigma_a(S)\ne0$ forces $\max Q\le2$, i.e.\ $Q\subseteq\{2\}$, i.e.\ $\{3,\dots,a\}\subseteq S$.
This gives $|S\cap\{2,\dots,a\}|\ge a-2$.

At equality $|S\cap\{2,\dots,a\}|=a-2$ we must have $S\cap\{2,\dots,a\}=\{3,\dots,a\}$ and
$Q=\{2\}$; then the only admissible $V$ is $V=\{2\}$ (it must contain $Q=\{2\}$ and have
$\max V\le2$), giving the single term $\sigma_a(S)=\varepsilon_2\,(y^{\mathsf T}p_2)(\pi_2^{\mathsf T}x)$,
which is nonzero (verification). So the minimiser is unique.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Only two facts are used for the bound: the rank--one collapse on $\W$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}, from the \emph{pair} pinch) and the left sign--kill
(Lemma~\ref{lem:leftbdry}, from the \emph{triple} pinch). Tridiagonality of the Gram
$\pi_j^{\mathsf T}p_{j'}$ --- which also follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:signkill}
(take $b=j$, $c=j'$ for $|j-j'|\ge2$, both away from the top) --- and the explicit
nonzero adjacent entries are needed only to display survivors, not for the lower bound.
This is strictly cleaner than the hook proof, where the full chip--firing apparatus was
deployed for the same conclusion.
\end{remark}

\section{The lower bound for far--junction--intact subsets}

\begin{theorem}[Intact--junction lower bound, unconditional]\label{thm:lb}
Let $\la=(2,2,1^m)$. If $S$ leaves every far junction intact and $c_S\ne0$, then
$|S|\ge\binom m2=\tfrac{\tau(\tau+1)}2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By (P2), $c_S=\prod_a\sigma_a(S)$ over the $n-3$ arcs; $c_S\ne0$ forces every
arc--scalar nonzero. The $\tau=m-1$ zero arcs have $a=3,4,\dots,n-3$ and occupy disjoint
position sets (interiors of distinct blocks). By Theorem~\ref{thm:reach} the arc with
parameter $a$ contributes at least $a-2$ insertions from its own positions, so
\[
|S|\;\ge\;\sum_{a=3}^{n-3}(a-2)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j\;=\;\binom m2\;=\;\frac{\tau(\tau+1)}2 . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

This upgrades Theorem~5.4 of \texttt{2026-05-27-order-law-offhook.tex} from
``verified'' to ``proved,'' for the whole infinite family $(2,2,1^m)$.

\section{The remaining gap: merged arcs are the generic survivor}

The bound above governs subsets that keep all far junctions intact. A subset may instead
place a $\Pm$ at a far--junction position, \emph{breaking} the pinch and \emph{merging}
two adjacent arcs. We make three honest points.

\paragraph{(1) The merged case is not a corner case --- it dominates.}
For $\la=(2,2,1,1,1)$ ($\tau=2$, predicted $\ord=3$) the critical grade $|S|=3$ has exactly
$14=C_4$ survivors, every one of value $q^8/(q+1)^{16}>0$. Of these, \textbf{$13$ break a
far junction}; only one ($S=\{$suffixes of the two left blocks$\}$) is far--junction
intact. Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:lb} alone, while it correctly forbids $|S|<3$ among intact
subsets, accounts for only $1/14$ of the actual critical survivors, and the broken case
must be controlled for the unconditional lower bound.

\paragraph{(2) The single--subspace restriction provably fails for merged arcs.}
The within--arc proof rests on a single inert top generator $T_{a+2}$ commuting with all
arc generators. A merged super--arc straddles two original tops $T_{a+1},T_{a+2}$ joined
by the broken junction's generator. Concretely, for $(2,2,1,1,1)$, breaking the shared
junction $(1,5)$ yields a super--arc with generator sequence $(3,2,1,5,4,3,2)$ and right
boundary $x\in\W_1\cap\W_6$; the defect generator $T_5$ is \emph{adjacent} to the top
$T_6$, so $\W_6$ is \emph{not} $T_5$--invariant and the product leaves $\W_6$. (We verified
that forcing the computation into $\W_6$ annihilates it spuriously.) No single
$q$--eigenspace is simultaneously invariant under all super--arc generators, so the
rank--one collapse has no single home.

\paragraph{(3) What is nonetheless certain.}
The full lower bound $\min\{|S|:c_S\ne0\}=3$ for $(2,2,1,1,1)$ is verified exhaustively over
all $\binom{21}{\le3}$ subsets at $q=5$ and $q=7/3$; combined with the $14$ equal positive
critical survivors this gives $\ord_{s=0}P_{(2,2,1,1,1)}=3$ outright. The structural
content proved here is the within--arc reach for the whole family; the merged--arc reach is
the precise --- and now sharply localised --- remaining obstruction.

\paragraph{Conjectural route for the merged case.}
The natural fix mirrors the hook merged--junction lemma (which used a \emph{single} reach
counter $R$ traversing a multi--block gap): replace the single inert--top restriction by a
\emph{sequence} of restrictions, one per block of the super--arc, glued at the broken
junctions. Equivalently, the defect generator $T_{a+1}$ (adjacent to both tops) should be
absorbed by a rank--two ``transfer'' across the junction, after which the triple--pinch
sign--kill again forces the bottom--vertex support. A clean statement: \emph{a super--arc
covering zero arcs with parameters $a_1<\cdots<a_p$ requires $\ge\sum_i(a_i-2)$ insertions
inside it, broken--junction positions included.} This is the off--hook analogue of
Lemma~6.3 of the hook paper and would complete the unconditional lower bound for
$(2,2,1^m)$.

\section{What is proved, and what remains}

\textbf{Proved (whole family $(2,2,1^m)$).}
\begin{itemize}
\item The pair and triple pinch identities (Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch}) and the far sign--kill
(Corollary~\ref{cor:signkill}).
\item The inert--top rank--one collapse (Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}): the hook structure,
restored on $\W_{a+2}$.
\item \textbf{The within--arc reach lemma} (Theorem~\ref{thm:reach}): a zero arc absorbs
$\ge a-2$ insertions, uniquely --- closing the first ``verified, not proved'' input of the
off--hook paper, via a one--line walk expansion powered by the triple--pinch sign--kill.
\item The intact--junction lower bound $\ord\ge\binom m2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:lb}),
unconditional.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Remaining (the one gap).} The merged (broken--junction) reach
(\S6), now shown to be the generic survivor configuration and provably outside the
single--subspace restriction; conjecturally settled by a glued, per--block restriction.
With it, the order law $\ord=\binom m2=\tfrac{\tau(\tau+1)}2$ would hold unconditionally for
the infinite off--hook family $(2,2,1^m)$ --- the first non--hook instance of the law. For
$(2,2,1,1)$ and $(2,2,1,1,1)$ the order law already holds outright by exhaustive
verification, with the structural reason for the lower bound now supplied.
