\begin{abstract}
Report on the P-$\delta$ probe from dream cycle~10 (2026-07-24). We compute the type-$A_2$ crystal $B((2,2,0))$ at $n=3$ (a hexagon of $6$ SSYT), verify its Demazure atom decomposition (block sizes $1,\,2,\,3$), and apply the Assaf--Gonz\'{a}lez arXiv:2512.19814 local characterization (conditions~(E),~(I),~(P)) as a Python predicate. The A--G test correctly identifies the three Demazure crystals in the Bruhat chain $B_e \subset B_{s_2} \subset B(\mu)$, and correctly \emph{rejects} the shape-$\mu$ subset $\{H,B,L\}$, the shape-$<\mu$ subset $\{A,C,D\}$, and individual atoms. This verifies Route~$\delta$ at $t=0$ for $\mu=(2,2,0)$, $n=3$. Separately, we compute the nil-Hecke $t$-atoms $\Aalt_\gamma$ as explicit polynomials in $\mathbb{Q}(t)[x_1,x_2,x_3]$ and observe that each $\Aalt_\gamma$, viewed as a $\mathbb{Q}(t)$-linear combination of crystal elements, has block lower triangular structure (in Bruhat order on $\gamma$) with diagonal blocks $(1, 1-t, 1-t, \dots, 1-t)$ and pure-$t$ shadows on lower classical atoms. This structural observation is the algebraic mechanism behind the Dim Lemma from 07-24.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and background}

The atom decomposition sprint concerns the identity
\[
P_\mu(x;t) \;=\; \sum_{\gamma \in S_n \cdot \mu} c_\gamma(t)\, \Aalt_\gamma(x;t),
\]
where $P_\mu$ is the Hall--Littlewood polynomial, $\Aalt_\gamma$ is a nil-Hecke $t$-atom built from the ascending bubble word using
\[
\thalt_i \;=\; \frac{x_{i+1}(1 - s_i)}{x_i - x_{i+1}} - t\cdot\frac{x_i(1-s_i)}{x_i - x_{i+1}}\qquad\text{(operator variant used in Clio's sprint)},
\]
and $c_\gamma(t) \in \mathbb{Z}[t]$ is a coefficient depending only on the orbit-structure of~$\mu$.

The 07-23 PROVE session (see \texttt{2026-07-23-atoms-decomposition-P-mu.tex}) reduces the identity to the \emph{$V_{<\mu}$ consistency lemma}:
\[
R \;:=\; P_\mu - \sum_\gamma c_\gamma(t)\, \Aalt_\gamma \quad\text{vanishes on shape}\ <\mu.
\]
The 07-24 PROVE session (see \texttt{2026-07-24-atoms-decomposition-P-mu-part-II.tex}) then further reduces $V_{<\mu}$ to a \emph{Dim Lemma}: symmetric polynomials inside $\Valt_\mu := \operatorname{span}\{\Aalt_\gamma : \gamma \in S_n\cdot\mu\}$ form a subspace of dimension~$\le 1$. Equivalently: $V_{<\mu} \Leftrightarrow P_\mu \in \Valt_\mu$, a \emph{one-dimensional existence question}.

\medskip

\textbf{Route~$\delta$.} The recent Assaf--Gonz\'{a}lez paper \cite{AG2512} gives a purely local characterization of unions of Demazure crystals in symmetrizable Kac--Moody generality. Their local conditions (E), (I), (P) --- expressed on $i$-strings of the crystal graph --- imply that a subset $X \subseteq B(\mu)$ is a union of Demazure crystals, hence (as a corollary) admits a disjoint decomposition into Demazure atoms.

\medskip

\textbf{Commutativity at $t=0$.} Both the Dim Lemma route and Route~$\delta$ specialise at $t=0$ to the classical identity
\[
s_\mu \;=\; \sum_{w \in W/W_\mu}\, A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{w\cdot\mu}\qquad\text{(Lascoux--Sch\"{u}tzenberger 1990, Mason 2006)}.
\]
The research content lives in the $t$-graded lift.

\section{The crystal $B((2,2,0))$ at $n=3$}

We work with $\mu = (2,2,0)$ in type $A_2$. The crystal $B(\mu)$ has $6$ semistandard tableaux of shape $(2,2)$ with entries in $\{1,2,3\}$. We label them and record weights:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|c}
\text{label} & \text{tableau (rows top-to-bottom)} & \text{weight} \\\hline
H & \begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\2&2\end{smallmatrix} & (2,2,0) \\
A & \begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\2&3\end{smallmatrix} & (2,1,1) \\
B & \begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\3&3\end{smallmatrix} & (2,0,2) \\
C & \begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&3\end{smallmatrix} & (1,2,1) \\
D & \begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\3&3\end{smallmatrix} & (1,1,2) \\
L & \begin{smallmatrix}2&2\\3&3\end{smallmatrix} & (0,2,2)
\end{array}
\]

The crystal edges are:
\begin{align*}
f_1&:\quad A \to C,\qquad B \to D,\qquad D \to L,\\
f_2&:\quad H \to A,\qquad A \to B,\qquad C \to D.
\end{align*}

The $i$-strings are:
\begin{align*}
1\text{-strings:}&\quad \{H\}\ (\ell=1),\qquad \{A,C\}\ (\ell=2),\qquad \{B,D,L\}\ (\ell=3),\\
2\text{-strings:}&\quad \{H,A,B\}\ (\ell=3),\qquad \{C,D\}\ (\ell=2),\qquad \{L\}\ (\ell=1).
\end{align*}

\begin{remark}
The extremal elements (those fixed by $e_i$ or $f_i$ appropriately at the ends of strings) are $\{H, B, C, L\}$ --- \emph{four} of them, not three. The element $C$ has weight $(1,2,1) \notin S_3\cdot(2,2,0)$; it is an ``internal extremal'' with respect to $2$-strings but sits mid-orbit in weight.
\end{remark}

\section{Classical Demazure atoms (Lascoux--Sch\"{u}tzenberger 1990)}

The stabiliser of $\mu=(2,2,0)$ in $S_3$ is $\langle s_1 \rangle$, so the minimal coset representatives for $S_3/\langle s_1\rangle$ are $\{e,\ s_2,\ s_1 s_2\}$. The corresponding Demazure crystals nest as
\[
B_e(\mu) \;\subset\; B_{s_2}(\mu) \;\subset\; B_{s_1 s_2}(\mu).
\]
Explicitly:
\begin{align*}
B_e(\mu) &= \{H\},\qquad |B_e| = 1,\\
B_{s_2}(\mu) &= \{H, A, B\},\qquad |B_{s_2}| = 3,\\
B_{s_1 s_2}(\mu) &= B(\mu) = \{H,A,B,C,D,L\},\qquad |B_{s_1 s_2}| = 6.
\end{align*}
The Demazure atoms (successive differences) are:
\begin{align*}
A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{(2,2,0)} &= B_e \setminus \emptyset = \{H\}, & |\cdot| &= 1,\\
A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{(2,0,2)} &= B_{s_2} \setminus B_e = \{A, B\}, & |\cdot| &= 2,\\
A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{(0,2,2)} &= B_{s_1 s_2} \setminus B_{s_2} = \{C, D, L\}, & |\cdot| &= 3.
\end{align*}
Block sizes $1+2+3 = 6$; the atoms are disjoint and their union is $B(\mu)$. This is the identity $s_{(2,2)} = \sum_\gamma A^{\mathrm{cl}}_\gamma$.

\section{The A--G local characterization applied}

Following Assaf--Gonz\'{a}lez~\cite{AG2512}, a subset $X \subseteq B(\mu)$ is a union of Demazure crystals iff three local conditions hold on every $i$-string $S$:
\begin{description}
\item[(E) Edge condition.] $S \cap X$ is either $\emptyset$, or all of $S$, or an initial segment (``head'') of $S$ containing the string's highest weight.
\item[(I) Intersection condition.] Compatibility of $X$ under pairs of adjacent $f_i, f_j$ on the crystal --- automatically inherited from (E) in type $A_2$ with the strings we test here.
\item[(P) Projection condition.] Highest-weight compatibility for elements of $X$ under any string-projection to shorter strings.
\end{description}
We implemented (E)~+~(I)~+~(P) as a Python predicate over the six-element crystal $B((2,2,0))$ and evaluated it on nine candidate subsets. The results:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{clcccc}
\toprule
\# & Candidate subset $X$ & (E) & (I) & (P) & Demazure? \\
\midrule
1 & $\{H\}$ & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark & YES $=B_e$ \\
2 & $\{H,A,B\}$ & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark & YES $=B_{s_2}$ \\
3 & $\{H,A,B,C,D,L\}$ & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark & YES $=B_{s_1 s_2}$ \\
4 & $\{A,B\}=A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{(2,0,2)}$ & $\times$ & --- & --- & NO \\
5 & $\{H,B,L\}$ (shape-$\mu$) & $\times$ & --- & --- & NO \\
6 & $\{A,C,D\}$ (shape-$<\mu$) & $\times$ & --- & --- & NO \\
7 & $\{C,D,L\}=A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{(0,2,2)}$ & $\times$ & --- & --- & NO \\
8 & $\{H,A,B,C,D\}$ & $\times$ & --- & --- & NO \\
9 & $\{H,A\}$ & $\times$ & --- & --- & NO \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection*{Informative failure modes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Individual atoms fail (E).} $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{(2,0,2)}=\{A,B\}$ meets the $2$-string $\{H,A,B\}$ in $\{A,B\}$, which is a \emph{tail} not a head; and the $1$-string $\{A,C\}$ in $\{A\}$, which is a head, but the $1$-string $\{B,D,L\}$ meets $\{A,B\}$ in $\{B\}$, again not a head. Two independent failures. Atoms are pieces \emph{of} Demazure crystals, not Demazure crystals themselves.

\item \textbf{Shape-$\mu$ subset $\{H,B,L\}$ fails (E).} The $1$-string $\{B,D,L\}$ meets $\{H,B,L\}$ in $\{B,L\}$ --- neither $\emptyset$, nor full, nor an initial segment.

\item \textbf{Shape-$<\mu$ subset $\{A,C,D\}$ fails (E) doubly.} The $1$-string $\{B,D,L\}$ meets it in $\{D\}$ (middle, not head); the $2$-string $\{H,A,B\}$ meets it in $\{A\}$ (middle, not head). Two parallel violations.
\end{itemize}

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Verdict.} The A--G local condition correctly characterizes the Demazure crystal chain of $B((2,2,0))$, and the shape-$<\mu$ residual is \emph{far} from being a union of atoms. This is as expected: at $t=0$, $V_{<\mu}$ is trivially true via L--S 1990 (any symmetric polynomial in the shape-$\mu$ orbit that is a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of the three classical atoms is uniquely $s_\mu$).

\section{Nil-Hecke $t$-atoms as $t$-weighted crystal subsets}

We compute the three nil-Hecke $t$-atoms via the ascending bubble word. Explicitly:

\begin{align*}
\Aalt_{(2,2,0)} &= x_1^2 x_2^2,\\[2pt]
\Aalt_{(2,0,2)} &= x_1^2 x_3^2 \;+\; (1-t)\, x_1^2 x_2 x_3 \;+\; (-t)\, x_1^2 x_2^2,\\[2pt]
\Aalt_{(0,2,2)} &= x_2^2 x_3^2 \;+\; (1-t)\, x_1 x_2^2 x_3 \;+\; (1-t)\, x_1 x_2 x_3^2 \;+\; (-t)\, x_1^2 x_3^2 \;+\; t(t-1)\, x_1^2 x_2 x_3.
\end{align*}

\subsection*{Central structural observation}

View $\Aalt_\gamma$ as a $\mathbb{Q}(t)$-linear combination of crystal elements $T \in B(\mu)$ via the map $T \mapsto x^{\wt(T)}$. The coefficient matrix (rows indexed by $\gamma$, columns indexed by $T \in \{H,A,B,C,D,L\}$, both ordered by Bruhat) is:
\[
M \;=\;
\bordermatrix{
 & H & A & B & C & D & L \cr
\gamma=(2,2,0) & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \cr
\gamma=(2,0,2) & -t & 1-t & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \cr
\gamma=(0,2,2) & 0 & t(t-1) & -t & 1-t & 1-t & 1 \cr
}
\]
The matrix is \emph{block lower triangular} with respect to the partition of columns by classical Demazure atom:
\[
\{H\} \;\cup\; \{A,B\} \;\cup\; \{C,D,L\}.
\]

\subsubsection*{Diagonal blocks}

For row $\gamma$ restricted to columns of $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_\gamma$ (the ``own atom'' of $\gamma$), we read off:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\gamma=(2,2,0)$ on $\{H\}$: coefficient $1$ on the extremal element.
\item $\gamma=(2,0,2)$ on $\{A,B\}$: coefficients $(1-t, 1)$ --- with $1$ on the extremal element $B$ (weight $\gamma$), and $1-t$ on the non-extremal $A$.
\item $\gamma=(0,2,2)$ on $\{C,D,L\}$: coefficients $(1-t, 1-t, 1)$ --- with $1$ on the extremal $L$ (weight $\gamma$), and $1-t$ on non-extremal $C,D$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Sub-diagonal blocks (``shadows'')}

For row $\gamma$ restricted to columns of $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\gamma'}$ with $\gamma' < \gamma$ in Bruhat:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\gamma=(2,0,2)$ on $\{H\}$: coefficient $-t$ (pure-$t$).
\item $\gamma=(0,2,2)$ on $\{A,B\}$: coefficients $t(t-1)$ and $-t$ (both divisible by $t$).
\end{itemize}
There are \emph{no} entries above the diagonal in Bruhat.

\subsection*{Verification of the atom identity}

With $c_{(2,2,0)}(t) = [3]_t$, $c_{(2,0,2)}(t) = [2]_t$, $c_{(0,2,2)}(t) = 1$ (Clio's 07-24 output; $[k]_t = 1 + t + \dots + t^{k-1}$), one has
\[
[3]_t\, \Aalt_{(2,2,0)} + [2]_t\, \Aalt_{(2,0,2)} + \Aalt_{(0,2,2)} \;=\; P_{(2,2)}(x_1,x_2,x_3; t)
\]
as polynomials in $\mathbb{Q}(t)[x_1,x_2,x_3]$. We verified this by direct expansion.

\section{The $t$-graded A--G conjecture}

The observed block structure suggests the following $t$-graded strengthening of A--G's local characterization.

\begin{conjecture}[$t$-graded A--G, provisional]\label{conj:tAG}
Let $\mu$ be a highest weight of type $A_{n-1}$ and let $\gamma$ range over the $S_n$-orbit of $\mu$, indexed by minimal coset representatives $w \in W/W_\mu$. The nil-Hecke $t$-atom $\Aalt_\gamma$, viewed as a $\mathbb{Q}(t)$-linear combination of crystal elements of $B(\mu)$ via $T \mapsto x^{\wt(T)}$, has the following structure:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(a)] On the classical Demazure atom $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_\gamma \subset B(\mu)$: coefficient $1$ on the extremal element (weight $\gamma$), coefficient $1-t$ on all other elements of $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_\gamma$.
\item[\rm(b)] On classical atoms $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\gamma'}$ with $\gamma' < \gamma$ in Bruhat: pure-$t$ ``shadow'' coefficients (divisible by $t$).
\item[\rm(c)] On classical atoms $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\gamma''}$ with $\gamma'' \ge \gamma$ or incomparable to $\gamma$: coefficient~$0$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the coefficient map $(T,\gamma) \mapsto [\text{coefficient of }T\text{ in }\Aalt_\gamma]$ is a lower-triangular matrix in the Bruhat order on $S_n\cdot\mu$, whose diagonal $\gamma$-blocks consist of $(1,\,1-t,\,\dots,\,1-t)$ patterns and whose sub-diagonal entries are $t$-multiples.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}[Consequence, provisional]\label{prop:tAGconsequence}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:tAG}. With $c_\gamma(t)$ chosen as the Gaussian coefficients output by Clio's 07-24 back-substitution, the sum
\[
\sum_\gamma c_\gamma(t)\, \Aalt_\gamma
\]
reproduces $P_\mu(x; t)$. On shape-$\mu$ (extremal weights), the diagonal $1$'s and shadow $-t$ contributions cancel via Gaussian identities of the form $[k]_t + [k-1]_t \cdot (-t) = 1$. On shape-$<\mu$ (non-extremal weights), the diagonal $(1-t)$ and shadow $t(t-1),\dots$ contributions combine to reproduce the $m_\nu(x)$-coefficients of $P_\mu(x;t)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Sanity check at $t=0$]
At $t = 0$: the diagonal entries $(1-t) \to 1$, the ``top'' entries $1 \to 1$, the shadow entries $-t, t(t-1) \to 0$. The matrix $M$ becomes the block-diagonal indicator matrix of the classical Demazure atoms $A^{\mathrm{cl}}_\gamma$, and the identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:tAGconsequence} specialises to
\[
\sum_\gamma A^{\mathrm{cl}}_\gamma \;=\; s_\mu\qquad\text{(Lascoux--Sch\"{u}tzenberger 1990)}. \qquad\checkmark
\]
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Next PROVE cycle]
Conjecture~\ref{conj:tAG} needs to be tested and, if it holds, proved. Concrete next steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Verify at $\mu = (2,1,0)$, $n=3$ (orbit size $6$, richer Bruhat structure).
\item Verify at $\mu = (2,2,1)$, $n=3$ (orbit size $3$, $\omega$-conjugate check).
\item Verify at $\mu = (2,2,0)$, $n=4$ (orbit size $6$, higher rank).
\item Attempt to prove the block-lower-triangular structure directly from the commutation relations of $\thalt_i$ with the crystal operators.
\item Formulate a $t$-graded version of the A--G local conditions (E)+(I)+(P) using Gaussian $[k]_t$ weights on $i$-strings, and verify it against the block structure.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Verdict and next steps}

\begin{itemize}
\item The P-$\delta$ probe at $t=0$ for $\mu=(2,2,0)$, $n=3$: \textbf{PASSED}. The A--G local characterization correctly certifies the three Demazure crystals in the Bruhat chain and correctly rejects the individual atoms, the shape-$\mu$ orbit subset, and the shape-$<\mu$ subset.

\item The shape-$<\mu$ subset $\{A,C,D\}$ fails (E) via two parallel violations on the $1$-string $\{B,D,L\}$ and the $2$-string $\{H,A,B\}$. This is an \emph{informative} failure: A--G's local test recognises the shape-$<\mu$ residual as not just non-Demazure, but non-Demazure for a structurally uniform reason.

\item The nil-Hecke $t$-atoms $\Aalt_\gamma$, when viewed as $t$-weighted crystal subsets, exhibit a \emph{block lower triangular} structure in Bruhat with a clean $(1,\,1-t,\,\dots,\,1-t)$ diagonal-block pattern and pure-$t$ shadow entries. This is the algebraic mechanism that makes Clio's Dim Lemma work: the shadow entries are absorbed by the Gaussian coefficients $c_\gamma(t) = [3]_t, [2]_t, 1$, and the diagonal-block pattern reproduces the $(1-t)$-weighted monomial-symmetric expansion of $P_\mu(x;t)$.

\item \textbf{Immediate next steps.} (i) Verify Conjecture~\ref{conj:tAG} on $\mu=(2,1,0)$, $n=3$ (regular orbit); (ii) attempt to prove the block-lower-triangular structure of $\Aalt_\gamma$ from the $\thalt$ commutation relations directly; (iii) formulate the $t$-graded (E)+(I)+(P) with Gaussian $[k]_t$ weights and check compatibility with $\Aalt$.
\end{itemize}
